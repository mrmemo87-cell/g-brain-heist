# Teacher pilot feedback instrument

**Status as of 2026-10-18:** prepared but **not distributed**. No completed responses or permissions to quote have been collected.

Invite 5–8 teachers, preferably across at least two schools. Keep this short enough to complete in about four minutes.

## Survey questions
1. How many lessons/classes did you use Brains Heist with over the last two weeks? (Number, broad context.)
2. On how many distinct days did you use it last week? (0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+)
3. What teaching decision did you make differently because of information you saw in Brains Heist? (Open text; no names or identifiable student data.)
4. How much time did Brains Heist save last week? (None / under 15 min / 15–30 / 31–60 / over 60 / not sure)
5. How disappointed would you be if the product were unavailable? (Very / Somewhat / Not disappointed)
6. Would you continue using Brains Heist next term? (Yes / Maybe / No; why?)
7. Could the school consider a paid pilot, and which role approves purchases? (Yes / Maybe / No / Don't know; job title only)
8. What is the biggest reason you do not use Brains Heist more often? (Open text)
9. Do you consent to anonymous quotation of your response in an accelerator/investor pitch? (Yes / No.) Using names/school identifiers requires separate permission.

## Draft outreach email

**Subject: Four minutes to help improve Brains Heist for teachers**

Hi,

Thank you for trying Brains Heist with your students. We are making the platform more useful for real teaching decisions and would appreciate a few minutes of candid feedback.

Would you answer the nine short questions above? We're particularly interested in what genuinely saves time, improves your visibility into learning, and stops you from using the platform more often.

Please avoid including students' names, private marks, or identifiable student information. We will not quote your feedback publicly without permission.

Thank you for helping improve Brains Heist.

## Evidence recording rules
- Record response date, role, school (internally only), weeks of actual usage, questions answered, and quote-consent status.
- Avoid contacting pupils about teacher feedback or asking teachers to disclose private pupil records.
- Preserve verbatim comments and negative feedback; don't manufacture testimonials.
- Report survey invitation count and response rate, not just positive answers.
- Share only consented, anonymized quotes externally.

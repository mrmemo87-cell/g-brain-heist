## Student task

Some people believe that schools should give students more time to explore subjects they choose themselves. Others believe that schools should spend that time teaching a common set of subjects to every student.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

**Instructions:** Write an essay in response to the task below. Explain your ideas and support them with relevant reasons and examples. Write at least 250 words. You have 40 minutes, including time to plan and check your work.

The 40-minute duration and 250-word minimum follow the official Task 2 format. This is one Task 2 snapshot, not the complete 60-minute Writing paper. Task 1 is absent. There is no full Writing band, overall band, calibration claim or persistent weakness label.

## Four criteria and teachable observations

These summaries are original explanatory wording, not copied band descriptors. Every demonstrated or development observation requires at least one exact, server-validated excerpt from the original essay. “More evidence needed” requires an explanation and is not scored as failure.

| Criterion | Review focus | Proposed canonical micro-skill mappings |
|---|---|---|
| Task Response | Coverage of both views; clear personal position; relevant explanation and support. Evaluate development, not keywords or a preferred opinion. | task-relevance; develop-ideas; support-examples |
| Coherence and Cohesion | Logical progression, purposeful paragraphs and clear reference between ideas. Do not count linking words as attainment. | logical-order; paragraphing; reference |
| Lexical Resource | Appropriate range, precision and natural collocation in context. Do not reward rare words regardless of accuracy. | precision; range; collocation |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | Sentence variety, accurate structure and punctuation; consider the effect of errors on communication. | sentence variety; grammatical accuracy; punctuation |

Full canonical codes appear in `draft-0.1.0.json`. The taxonomy registry is `bh-english-core-v1`. Review mappings explicitly before publishing. Frozen submission provenance preserves the rubric version, policy and exact reviewed form hash. Reviewed Task 2 observations always retain low confidence because one short, uncalibrated task does not establish full Writing readiness.

The student sees “Awaiting teacher review” until a permitted teacher saves feedback. Teacher feedback is task-specific and supported by original evidence. Corrections append a new review and preserve earlier reviews. Delivery incidents require a teacher comment before feedback can be saved. Revisions are separate coached practice, tied to the review they used; they never replace the original assessment or measure independent improvement.

## What is reused from the Writing Hub

- Focused prompt/editor and visible word count.
- Original submission preserved separately from revisions.
- Exact-text evidence validation and clear practical feedback.
- Scoped teacher judgement and traceable review history.

The IELTS attempt engine supplies server deadlines, monotonic autosaving, recovery, interruption records, idempotent submission and entitlement checks. No Cambridge 0–20 score, grade-based profile, keyword heuristic, browser-only timer, automatic essay deletion on tab switch or automatic AI “verified” claim is imported. AI scoring is not part of this first release; teacher review is the authority.

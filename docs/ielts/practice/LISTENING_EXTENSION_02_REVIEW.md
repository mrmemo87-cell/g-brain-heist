### Exact conversation script

**Receptionist:** Good morning, Bellmere Arts Centre. How can I help?

**Caller:** Hello. I'd like to book the beginner pottery workshop. The leaflet says Sunday. Is that still right?

**Receptionist:** That was the original plan, but the tutor is away on Sunday. We've moved it to Saturday. The website now has the correct day.

**Caller:** Saturday suits me. I finish work early that morning. Does it still start at ten?

**Receptionist:** A little earlier, actually. We need time to prepare the clay, so the workshop begins at nine forty. Please be ready then; ten o'clock is when the first demonstration ends.

**Caller:** I'll make a note. And is the fee eighteen pounds, as advertised? I'm a student.

**Receptionist:** Eighteen is the standard fee. With a current student card, you'll pay fourteen pounds. That includes the clay and firing your finished piece.

**Caller:** Lovely. I've been to the centre before. Shall I come through the main entrance?

**Receptionist:** There's a market outside it that morning. Use the west gate instead. A volunteer there will direct you to the studio.

**Caller:** Do I need to bring an apron, or any tools?

**Receptionist:** We supply those. Bring a sketchbook so you can draw ideas for your piece. You don't need to buy any special equipment.

**Caller:** My classmate might come too. The leaflet mentions twenty places. Are there still spaces?

**Receptionist:** There are spaces, yes, but we've reduced the group to sixteen so everyone gets enough help. That is the maximum now. I can reserve two places if your classmate confirms today.

**Caller:** I'll check with her and call back. Thanks for explaining everything.

**Receptionist:** You're welcome. We look forward to seeing you.

## L4: Agree the research plan: community food waste

Audio: L4_practice_v1.mp3 · 174.3 seconds

A quieter conversational passage in a library setting. Fully voiced speech, consistent intelligibility and matched playback level. No true whisper, background noise or answer masking.

**Learner instruction:** Complete the notes. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer. You may pause and replay. Listen for the final confirmed detail.

### Questions and key

| No. | Note to complete | Accepted answers | Primary skill | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agreed project topic | food waste | Explicit detail | Public transport was rejected as too broad; they agree on food waste. |
| 2 | Method of collecting information | interviews | Explicit detail | They replace the questionnaire with interviews to explore reasons. |
| 3 | Participant group | shop owners | Explicit detail | Customers were considered, but shop owners know about unsold food. |
| 4 | Minimum number of interviews | 12 / twelve | Numerical information | Fifteen would be useful; the tutor requires at least twelve. |
| 5 | Agreed meeting day | Thursday | Dates and times | Wednesday conflicts with Leo's laboratory session; Thursday is agreed. |

### Exact conversation script

**Mia:** Let's keep our voices down a little. This is the quiet study area. Did you look at the tutor's comments on our project proposal?

**Leo:** Yes. Our first idea, public transport, was too broad. I think food waste would be easier to investigate locally.

**Mia:** Agreed. Food waste it is. We can find out what happens to unsold food. Were you still thinking of using a questionnaire?

**Leo:** I was, but the tutor wants us to explore people's reasons, not just count responses. Let's use interviews instead. We can ask follow-up questions when an answer isn't clear.

**Mia:** That makes sense. At first I thought we could talk to customers, but they probably wouldn't know how much gets thrown away.

**Leo:** Exactly. Shop owners can explain that, so they should be our participants. We can ask how they plan their orders and deal with anything left over.

**Mia:** How many interviews do we need? I wrote fifteen in our first draft.

**Leo:** The tutor said we must complete at least twelve. Fifteen would be useful, but twelve is the minimum we need to plan for.

**Mia:** Fine. Let's divide the work evenly. Could we meet on Wednesday to check the interview questions before we contact anyone?

**Leo:** I have a laboratory session then. Thursday works for me, and it still gives us enough time.

**Mia:** Thursday is good. Shall we meet back here in the library?

**Leo:** We need to practise asking the questions aloud, so this quiet area won't work. I've booked Room Four in the study centre. Let's meet there.

**Mia:** Great. I'll bring the draft questions. Before we leave, let's check that none of them tells people what answer we expect.

**Leo:** Good idea. We want to hear their experience, even if it doesn't match what we predicted.

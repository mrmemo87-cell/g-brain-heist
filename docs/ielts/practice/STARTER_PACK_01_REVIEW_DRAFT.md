## R1 — Reading practice: a statement is not always a match
ID: bh-ielts-practice-r-detail-001 | version 0.1.0
Proposed targets: paraphrase recognition; detail discrimination; True/False/Not Given reasoning.
Purpose: guided practice | Suggested time: 12 minutes, untimed flexibility.
Why: practise separating what a passage says, contradicts, and leaves unknown.
Success description: explain each answer using the relevant words, or explain which information is absent. This describes the exercise, not a mastery threshold.

### Student material
A university library piloted a quiet study room for six weeks. Students booked a place online, and each booking lasted up to ninety minutes. During the pilot, the room opened at 8 a.m. on weekdays; weekend opening hours stayed unchanged. Staff moved the printers into the corridor after students complained about noise. In the final week, most survey respondents said the room helped them concentrate. However, only thirty-two students returned the survey, so the library decided to collect more feedback before extending the scheme.

TRUE: agrees with the passage. FALSE: contradicts it. NOT GIVEN: the passage does not establish it.
1. Students could reserve a place using the internet.
2. A booking had to last exactly ninety minutes.
3. The pilot introduced longer weekend opening hours.
4. Printers were moved because they disturbed students.
5. Every student who used the room completed the survey.
6. Students preferred the quiet room to studying at home.

Before answering, underline a supporting phrase. For NOT GIVEN, name the missing comparison or fact.
Worked example: “The pilot ran for six weeks.” TRUE: the passage explicitly states this.

### Teacher-only key and feedback
1 TRUE — “booked a place online”.
2 FALSE — “up to” permits shorter bookings.
3 FALSE — weekend hours “stayed unchanged”.
4 TRUE — moved after noise complaints.
5 NOT GIVEN — thirty-two responses; total users and their identities are unspecified.
6 NOT GIVEN — no home-study comparison.
Discuss 2/3 as qualifiers and 5/6 as missing information. Do not teach “not exactly the same words means false”.

## R2 — Reading independent check: evidence before assumption
ID: bh-ielts-check-r-detail-001 | version 0.1.0
Proposed targets: as R1; mapping pending.
Purpose: fresh independent check | Suggested time: 8 minutes; record actual conditions.
Student instructions: no hints or feedback until submission. Read the passage and answer TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN. Then quote a short supporting phrase or describe what is missing.

### Student material
A community centre introduced an equipment-lending service in March. Adults could borrow gardening tools after attending a short safety session. Members paid no borrowing fee, but left a refundable deposit. Tools had to be returned within seven days. During April, the centre received more requests for drills than for any other item. Because several tools came back dirty, staff introduced a cleaning checklist in May. The centre plans to review the service in September before deciding whether to buy more equipment.

1. A safety session was required before adults borrowed tools.
2. Members paid a fee that was never returned.
3. Borrowers could keep tools for two weeks.
4. Drills were the most requested item in April.
5. The cleaning checklist began when the service opened.
6. The centre will definitely purchase more equipment in September.

### Teacher-only key
1 TRUE — “after attending” establishes prerequisite.
2 FALSE — no borrowing fee; deposit refundable.
3 FALSE — seven-day limit.
4 TRUE — more requests than any other item.
5 FALSE — March opening, May checklist.
6 NOT GIVEN — decision pending; future purchase is not established.
Record answer and explanation separately. Review errors by construct; one total cannot establish every target. This passage is a different context with different demands, not calibrated equivalent to R1.

## L1 — Listening practice: follow the correction
ID: bh-ielts-practice-l-correction-001 | version 0.1.0
Proposed targets: numerical information; dates/times; distractor resistance.
Purpose: guided practice | Suggested time: 12 minutes.
Audio status: owner-approved recording available in private, versioned storage for the named-user pilot. Exact hashes and canonical mappings: LISTENING_PILOT_01.json. Public rights review and device pilot remain pending.
Proposed practice delivery: 30 seconds to inspect the form; play at natural pace; pause/replay allowed; 15 seconds to finish. Record assistance and replay. Timing requires academic/audio review, not assumed approval.

### Student material
Complete the booking notes. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.
1. Workshop day: ______
2. Starting time: ______
3. Meeting room: ______
4. Student fee: £______
5. Bring a: ______
6. Maximum group size: ______
Strategy: listen for the final confirmed detail. “Actually”, “instead” and “sorry” may signal a correction.

### Teacher-only audio script
Staff: Hello, are you calling about the photography workshop?
Student: Yes. I thought it was on Saturday.
Staff: That was the original plan, but it's now on Sunday.
Student: Does it still start at ten?
Staff: We advertised ten, but the instructor needs another half hour. We'll begin at ten thirty.
Student: Where should I go?
Staff: Room twelve was booked already, so we've moved to room twenty.
Student: The poster says the fee is eighteen pounds.
Staff: That's the standard price. Students pay twelve pounds if they show their student card.
Student: Should I bring my camera?
Staff: We'll provide cameras. Bring a notebook so you can write down the settings.
Student: How many people will be there?
Staff: We considered taking sixteen, but ten is the maximum. That gives everyone enough time with the equipment.

### Teacher-only key
1 Sunday; 2 10:30 / ten thirty / 10.30; 3 20 / twenty; 4 12 / twelve; 5 notebook; 6 10 / ten.
Approved normalization must handle case, whitespace and reviewed equivalent time representations. Keys and alternatives need human review; do not accept ambiguous “ten” for item 2.
After first attempt, allow replay and explain the change cues. Preserve first and supported responses separately; rehearsal is not an independent check.

### Student material
Complete the visit notes. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER.
1. Visit day: ______
2. Departure time: ______
3. Meeting point: ______
4. Student ticket: £______
5. Bring a: ______

### Teacher-only audio script
Organiser: Here's the final information about the museum visit. The school calendar still says Tuesday, but the museum is closed that day, so we're going on Thursday. We first planned to leave at eight fifteen. The coach company has asked us to be ready earlier, and the confirmed departure is eight. Don't wait at the main entrance as you did last time. Meet at the side gate. The general ticket costs fourteen pounds, but our student tickets are nine pounds each. Lunch is included, and you won't need a packed meal. Please bring a pencil for the sketching activity. Finally, we hoped to offer thirty places, but the coach has only twenty-four available seats, so that's our limit.

### Teacher-only key
1 Thursday; 2 8:00 / eight / 8 / 08:00; 3 side gate; 4 9 / nine; 5 pencil; 6 24 / twenty-four / twenty four.

## W1 — Writing practice: make an example do useful work
ID: bh-ielts-practice-w-support-001 | version 0.1.0
Proposed targets: idea development; evidence/example relevance.
Purpose: guided Task 2 micro-skill practice | Suggested time: 15 minutes.
Prompt: Some people think schools should teach practical life skills alongside academic subjects. Develop one paragraph supporting that position.
Task-specific requirement: one paragraph, suggested 80–120 words; guidance, not an automatic rejection threshold.
Include a clear point, an explanation, a specific plausible example and an explanation of how the example supports the point. Do not invent research statistics.
Teacher-only scaffold: “A useful life skill is… This matters because… For example… This shows…”. Scaffolding must be recorded.
Success description: the example is specific and the final explanation connects it to the paragraph's claim.
Teacher feedback: identify one effective link, quote one unclear link, and give one revision instruction.
No model paragraph is required before the initial response; supplied models count as assistance.

## S1 — Speaking practice: explain beyond the first answer
ID: bh-ielts-practice-s-development-001 | version 0.1.0
Proposed targets: topic development; discourse organization; sustained turn.
Purpose: guided speaking practice | Suggested time: 10 minutes.
Question: Describe a place where you enjoy studying. Explain what it is like and why it helps you.
Prepare short notes, then speak for approximately 45–90 seconds. This target suits the exercise and is not a universal submission minimum.
Teacher prompts, if needed: “What makes it suitable?” “Can you give an example?” “Has your preference changed?”
Success description: go beyond naming the place by explaining a reason and illustrating it with a relevant detail.
Record prompts, preparation, recording/live observation and feedback. Do not award a pronunciation rating from a transcript.

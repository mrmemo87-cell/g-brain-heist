## Six short texts: who is it for, and why? · Guided practice

*Read the six short texts. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question.*

Text 1. Residents of Block C: The lift will be out of service from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Thursday 14 November while engineers replace the cables. Please plan any deliveries for another day. Residents who need help with the stairs should call the building office before Wednesday so that we can arrange assistance.

Text 2. Are your weekends too quiet? Join Riverside Climbing Wall’s Saturday Starter Club! No experience needed: our instructors will have you reaching the top by lunchtime. The first session is half price for anyone who brings a friend. Book online today, as places are limited.

Text 3. Abstract. This study compared the reading scores of 240 pupils in schools with and without daily silent-reading periods over two academic years. Pupils with daily reading periods gained, on average, 0.3 standard deviations more than the comparison group. We discuss limitations, including differences in school size, and recommend a larger randomised trial.

Text 4. Dear Ms Okafor, Thank you for your order of 12 March. Unfortunately, the walking boots you selected are no longer available in size 39. We have refunded the full amount of £64 to your card, and it should appear within five working days. We are sorry for any inconvenience and hope to serve you again.

Text 5. I used to think composting was only for people with big gardens. Then I tried a small worm bin on my balcony. It doesn’t smell, it takes ten minutes a week, and my tomato plants have never been healthier. If you live in a flat and throw away vegetable peelings every day, please give it a try. You might be surprised.

Text 6. Before your first shift: 1. Collect your ID badge from reception. 2. Watch the 20-minute safety video in Room 3. 3. Sign the form at the end of the video and hand it to your supervisor. You may not enter the kitchen until all three steps are complete.

1. What is the main purpose of Text 1?  A  To apologise for a broken lift · B  To warn residents about a temporary disruption and how to prepare · C  To advertise a delivery service · D  To explain how lifts are repaired — **B**
2. Who is Text 2 mainly written for?  A  Experienced climbers · B  Instructors looking for work · C  People who have never climbed · D  Parents of young children — **C**
3. Who is the intended audience of Text 3?  A  Researchers and education professionals · B  Pupils choosing a school · C  Parents of young children · D  School librarians looking for books — **A**
4. What is the main purpose of Text 4?  A  To persuade the customer to buy different boots · B  To ask the customer for her card details · C  To complain about a late payment · D  To tell a customer that an item is unavailable and that she has been refunded — **D**
5. What is the writer’s main purpose in Text 5?  A  To persuade people in flats to try composting · B  To describe the history of composting · C  To complain about a bad smell · D  To give instructions for growing tomatoes — **A**
6. Who is Text 6 written for?  A  Customers of a restaurant · B  New kitchen staff · C  Safety inspectors · D  Reception workers — **B**

Teacher notes:

```
1 B: a notice to residents about a temporary closure, with what to do (plan deliveries, call for help). Not an apology (A).
2 C: “No experience needed … Starter Club”. The reader is addressed as someone new to climbing; instructors (B) are mentioned, not addressed.
3 A: an abstract with standard deviations, limitations and a recommended trial is written for researchers and education professionals.
4 D: a business letter that informs (unavailable) and confirms a refund. The apology is part of it, not the main job.
5 A: personal story, then “please give it a try” to people in flats. Tomatoes (D) are evidence, not instructions.
6 B: “Before your first shift … supervisor … kitchen”. Written for new kitchen workers.
Teach: for each text, say who “you” is and what the reader should do or know after reading.
```

## Six short texts: purpose and reader check · Fresh check

*Read the six short texts. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question. Work on your own, without notes, a dictionary or help. Tell your teacher if anything interrupted you.*

Text 1. Library users: From 1 December, overdue books will no longer be charged a daily fine. Instead, borrowers who keep a book more than four weeks late will not be able to borrow again until it is returned. We hope this change will encourage everyone to bring books back, rather than staying away because of fines.

Text 2. Tired of the same old lunch? Try the new Green Bowl at Café Lumo: fresh grains, roasted vegetables and our famous lemon dressing, all for £6.50. Show your student card for a free drink until the end of the month.

Text 3. Hey Sam! I can’t believe it’s been a year since you moved. Guess what? I finally passed my driving test (third time lucky!). Mum says you’re coming back for the summer, so let’s do a road trip to the lakes. I’ll drive, you bring the snacks. Write back soon!

Text 4. Warning: this product contains nuts. Not suitable for children under three because of small parts. Keep in a cool, dry place. Once opened, eat within two days.

Text 5. The council’s proposal to close the Elm Street swimming pool must be rejected. The pool is the only one within walking distance for 6,000 residents, and its lessons have taught hundreds of local children to swim. Closing it to save £80,000 a year is a false economy. Write to your councillor before the vote on 3 March.

Text 6. Welcome to the Science Museum audio guide. To begin, press the green button. Each exhibit has a number on a blue sign; enter the number and press play. You can change the volume with the side buttons. Please return the device to the desk by the exit.

1. What is the main purpose of Text 1?  A  To announce a change to a rule and explain the reason for it · B  To ask for donations of books · C  To complain about borrowers · D  To advertise new opening hours — **A**
2. Which group does Text 2 make a special effort to attract?  A  Café staff · B  Students · C  Farmers · D  Restaurant critics — **B**
3. Who is the intended reader of Text 3?  A  A driving examiner · B  An employer · C  A close friend · D  A stranger who answered an advert — **C**
4. What is the main purpose of Text 4?  A  To describe how the product was made · B  To persuade people to buy the product · C  To tell a story about the product · D  To give buyers safety and storage information — **D**
5. What is the writer’s main purpose in Text 5?  A  To persuade readers to oppose the closure of the pool · B  To report the result of a vote · C  To advertise swimming lessons · D  To explain the council’s budget — **A**
6. What is the main purpose of Text 6?  A  To describe the history of the museum · B  To tell visitors how to use the audio guide · C  To sell museum tickets · D  To warn visitors about dangerous exhibits — **B**

Teacher notes:

```
1 A: announces the end of fines and the new rule, then gives the reason (“We hope this change will encourage …”).
2 B: the general advert adds a special offer for anyone who shows a student card.
3 C: informal greeting, shared family news and plans: a close friend.
4 D: label information (allergy warning, age limit, storage).
5 A: “must be rejected … Write to your councillor”: persuasion. The vote has not happened yet (B).
6 B: step-by-step instructions for a device.
Different texts from R26; not a calibrated equivalent form.
```

## Streetlights: cause, contrast and result · Guided practice · Recognise logical relationships

*Read the passage. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question.*

Ten years ago, the town of Marlow Bridge replaced its old orange streetlights with white LED lamps. The main reason was cost: the new lamps use about half as much electricity. However, the change was not popular at first. Many residents said the white light was too harsh, and some complained that it kept them awake. As a result, the council fitted shields to lamps near bedroom windows and lowered their brightness after midnight. Although these changes reduced the savings slightly, complaints fell sharply. The council also found an unexpected benefit. Because the LED lamps last much longer, workers now replace them every twelve years instead of every four, so fewer roads have to be closed for repairs. If the lamps had not been dimmed, the council estimates that savings would have been ten per cent higher.

1. Why did the town replace its streetlights?  A  Because residents complained about them · B  To reduce the cost of electricity · C  To reduce the number of road closures · D  Because the orange lamps were too bright — **B**
2. What happened because residents complained?  A  The council removed the LED lamps · B  Electricity costs doubled · C  Shields were fitted and the lamps were dimmed at night · D  Workers replaced the lamps more often — **C**
3. What does the sentence beginning “Although” show?  A  The changes cost some savings but reduced complaints · B  The changes increased the savings · C  The changes caused more complaints · D  The changes were made before the new lamps arrived — **A**
4. Why do fewer roads have to be closed for repairs?  A  The lamps are dimmed after midnight · B  Residents complain less · C  The council has fewer workers · D  The LED lamps last longer — **D**
5. According to the council, what would have happened if the lamps had not been dimmed?  A  The savings would have been higher · B  More lamps would have broken · C  Complaints would have fallen faster · D  Roads would have been closed less often — **A**
6. What does “As a result” introduce?  A  A contrast with the previous sentence · B  A consequence of the complaints · C  An example of the savings · D  A condition for the change — **B**

Teacher notes:

```
1 B — “The main reason was cost” (A is a later effect, not the reason).
2 C — “As a result, the council fitted shields … and lowered their brightness”.
3 A — concession: a small loss of savings, a large fall in complaints.
4 D — “Because the LED lamps last much longer … so fewer roads”.
5 A — third conditional: savings would have been ten per cent higher.
6 B — “As a result” follows the complaints.
Teach: underline each linking word and draw an arrow from cause to effect.
```

## Late library: why, despite and if · Fresh check · Recognise logical relationships

*Read the passage. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question. Work on your own, without notes, a dictionary or help. Tell your teacher if anything interrupted you.*

Last year, a public library in a coastal city began opening until 10 p.m. on weekdays. The decision followed a survey which showed that many adults could not visit during the day because of work. Extending the hours was expensive, since extra security staff had to be employed. Nevertheless, the library judged the change worthwhile. Evening visits rose steadily, and borrowing of study books increased by a third. Despite this success, the late opening on Fridays was cancelled after six months because very few people came. The library now plans to use the Friday budget for weekend reading clubs for children, provided that enough volunteers can be found.

1. Why did the library start opening late?  A  Security staff asked for longer hours · B  Borrowing of books had fallen · C  Many adults could not visit during the day because of work · D  Children needed reading clubs — **C**
2. Why was extending the hours expensive?  A  More books had to be bought · B  Extra security staff were needed · C  The building needed more heating · D  Volunteers had to be paid — **B**
3. What does “Nevertheless” show?  A  The library continued with the change despite the cost · B  The cost was lower than expected · C  The survey had been wrong · D  Evening visits fell — **A**
4. What was one result of the late opening?  A  Weekend reading clubs began · B  Security costs fell · C  Friday visits rose · D  Borrowing of study books increased — **D**
5. Why was the Friday late opening cancelled?  A  Very few people came · B  Security staff were not available · C  The volunteers left · D  The survey recommended it — **A**
6. The weekend reading clubs will start only if …  A  Friday visits increase · B  the council pays more · C  enough volunteers are found · D  adults ask for them — **C**

## Honeybees: what does it refer to? · Guided practice · Track reference

*Read the passage. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question.*

Beekeepers in many countries have reported losing large numbers of honeybees each winter. Scientists have suggested several causes for this. One is a tiny parasite that attaches itself to bees and weakens them. Another is the loss of wild flowers, which means bees have fewer sources of food. Some farmers have responded by planting strips of flowers along the edges of their fields. These provide food from spring to autumn, and they also attract other insects that eat crop pests. A third cause may be the chemicals used to protect crops; although they are tested for safety, their combined effect on bees is less well understood. Researchers say that no single factor explains the losses. Instead, it is the combination that matters.

1. In “several causes for this”, “this” refers to …  A  the loss of honeybees each winter · B  the work of scientists · C  a tiny parasite · D  the cold weather — **A**

4. In “These provide food from spring to autumn”, “These” refers to …  A  the farmers · B  the fields · C  the crop pests · D  the strips of flowers — **D**
5. In “although they are tested for safety”, “they” refers to …  A  the bees · B  the chemicals · C  the crops · D  the researchers — **B**
6. In “it is the combination that matters”, the combination is of …  A  the different causes · B  flowers and insects · C  chemicals and crops · D  beekeepers and scientists — **A**

Teacher notes:

```
1 A — “this” = the winter losses.
2 B — the parasite attaches itself.

## Teenage sleep: why does the writer mention it? · Guided practice · Infer the writer's purpose

*Read the passage. Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D, for each question.*

Many parents complain that their teenage children stay up late and are difficult to wake in the morning. It is easy to see this as laziness, but research suggests another explanation. During adolescence, the body releases the sleep hormone melatonin about two hours later than in childhood. In other words, teenagers are not choosing to feel awake at night; their bodies are. Some schools have moved the start of the school day from 7.30 to 8.30 a.m. In those schools, attendance improved and fewer students fell asleep in class. Of course, a later start creates problems for bus timetables and after-school sports. Even so, the evidence suggests that school times should take teenagers’ biology into account.

1. Why does the writer mention parents’ complaints?  A  To show that parents are right · B  To introduce a common view that the writer will question · C  To criticise teenagers · D  To describe a research method — **B**
2. Why does the writer mention melatonin?  A  To explain why teenagers feel awake late at night · B  To recommend a medicine · C  To compare the diets of children and adults · D  To show that sleep is unimportant — **A**
3. What is the purpose of the sentence beginning “In other words”?  A  To introduce a new topic · B  To disagree with the research · C  To restate the scientific point in simple terms · D  To give an example of a school — **C**
4. Why does the writer mention schools that start at 8.30?  A  To show that bus timetables are a problem · B  To criticise attendance rules · C  To describe after-school sports · D  To give evidence that a later start can help — **D**
5. Why does the writer mention bus timetables and after-school sports?  A  To admit that a later start has practical difficulties · B  To argue that schools should start earlier · C  To give more evidence of the benefits · D  To change the topic to sport — **A**
6. What is the writer’s main purpose?  A  To describe how hormones work · B  To argue that school times should reflect teenagers’ sleep patterns · C  To complain about lazy teenagers · D  To compare schools in different countries — **B**

Teacher notes:

```
1 B — sets up the “laziness” view, then “but research suggests another explanation”.
2 A.
3 C — “In other words” restates.
4 D — evidence: attendance improved.
5 A — concession “Of course …”.
6 B — final sentence states the position.
Teach: purpose verbs — introduce, explain, illustrate, support, concede, conclude.

## Tourism in small towns: the writer’s views · Guided practice · Identify viewpoint and attitude

## Electric scooters: the writer’s views · Fresh check · Identify viewpoint and attitude

Rental electric scooters have appeared in many cities, and opinions about them are divided. I think they are, on balance, a positive development. Critics say that scooters left on pavements are dangerous for pedestrians, and I agree that this is a real problem. However, banning scooters, as some cities have done, seems to me an overreaction. Requiring riders to leave scooters in marked parking areas would solve most of the difficulty.

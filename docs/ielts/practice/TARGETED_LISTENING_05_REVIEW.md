## L5 · Rent a garden plot: Greenway Allotments

`bh-ielts-targeted-listening-l5-v1` · Guided practice · focus **Distractor resistance** · voices: Officer = James, Caller = Paula

**Student instructions:** You will hear a telephone conversation between a woman and an officer at a community garden. Answer questions 1 to 6. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer. You may pause or replay for practice.

**Tip shown with the task:** Each answer is mentioned after a wrong option. Wait for the speaker to correct, update or reject the first detail before you write.

**Transcript**

> **Officer:** Good afternoon, Greenway Allotments. How can I help?  
> **Caller:** Hi. I'd like to rent a garden plot. A friend told me the small plots are thirty pounds a year.  
> **Officer:** They were, but the price went up in January. A small plot is now thirty-five pounds a year.  
> **Caller:** That's fine. Could I have one near the car park? I'll be carrying tools.  
> **Officer:** The car park plots have all gone, I'm afraid. The nearest free ones are beside the greenhouse. It's only a short walk.  
> **Caller:** Next to the greenhouse, then. When could I start?  
> **Officer:** New tenants usually begin on the first of March, but the paths are being repaired this year, so you'll start on the fifteenth of March.  
> **Caller:** OK. Do I need my own spade and fork?  
> **Officer:** No, we lend those free of charge. What you do need is a padlock for the tool box on your plot.  
> **Caller:** A padlock. Right. Is there anyone I can ask for advice? I've never grown vegetables.  
> **Officer:** Our manager, Mr Hughes, used to help beginners, but he's retired now. Ask Joanna Price. She's our volunteer adviser. That's P, R, I, C, E.  
> **Caller:** Joanna Price. And how should I pay?  
> **Officer:** We don't take cash any more, and the card machine is broken, so please pay by bank transfer.  
> **Caller:** Bank transfer. Thanks very much.  

**Questions and key**

1. Yearly cost of a small plot: £
   - **Key:** 35 / thirty-five / thirty five — Thirty pounds was the old price; it is now thirty-five. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Numerical information.
2. Plot location: next to the
   - **Key:** greenhouse / the greenhouse — The car park plots have gone; the free plots are beside the greenhouse. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Explicit detail.
3. Start date
   - **Key:** 15 March / 15th March / March 15 / March 15th / fifteenth March — The usual date is 1 March; this year it is the fifteenth. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Dates and times.
4. Must bring a
   - **Key:** padlock — A spade and fork are lent free; the caller needs a padlock. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Explicit detail.
5. Adviser's surname
   - **Key:** Price — Mr Hughes has retired; the adviser is Joanna Price. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Explicit detail.
6. Payment method
   - **Key:** bank transfer / transfer — Cash is no longer taken and the card machine is broken. Primary: Distractor resistance; supporting: Explicit detail.

## L7 · Report on a field trip: the wetland centre

`bh-ielts-targeted-listening-l7-v1` · Guided practice · focus **Paraphrase recognition** · voices: Tutor = Marlene, Daniel = James

**Student instructions:** You will hear a conversation between a tutor and a student, Daniel, about a field trip. Answer questions 1 to 6. Choose the correct letter, A, B or C, for each question. You may pause or replay for practice.

**Tip shown with the task:** The correct option uses different words from the speaker. Options that repeat words you hear are often wrong; match the meaning, not the words.

**Transcript**

> **Tutor:** So, Daniel, you've just come back from the wetland centre. How was the trip?  
> **Daniel:** Better than I expected. I'd assumed it would just be birdwatching, but most of the day was about how the water is managed.  
> **Tutor:** And the guide? Was she helpful?  
> **Daniel:** Very. She didn't give us a long talk at the start. Instead, she stopped every few minutes and got us to work things out for ourselves.  
> **Tutor:** Good. The centre had a problem with flooding a few years ago, didn't it?  
> **Daniel:** Yes. The old walls kept breaking, so instead of building stronger ones, they let part of the land turn back into marsh. Now that soaks up the extra water.  
> **Tutor:** Interesting. What about visitor numbers?  
> **Daniel:** They've gone up, but not because of advertising. The guide said it's mainly families who came once and then told their friends.  
> **Tutor:** Did you manage to collect data for your project?  
> **Daniel:** Some. My plan was to count insects, but it was so cold there weren't many about, so I measured the water temperature at different points instead.  
> **Tutor:** Sensible. And what will you do next?  
> **Daniel:** I'd like to go back in spring, when it's warmer, and repeat the measurements, so I can compare the two seasons.  

**Questions and key**

1. What surprised Daniel about the trip?
   - A  There were more birds than he expected.
   - B  It focused mainly on controlling water.
   - C  The weather was worse than forecast.
   - **Key:** B — "most of the day was about how the water is managed" = controlling water. Birds are mentioned but were not the focus. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
2. How did the guide teach the group?
   - A  She gave a detailed introduction.
   - B  She showed them a short film.
   - C  She encouraged them to find answers themselves.
   - **Key:** C — "got us to work things out for ourselves" = find answers themselves. She did NOT give a long talk. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
3. How did the centre deal with flooding?
   - A  It built higher walls.
   - B  It allowed some land to become wet again.
   - C  It sent water to a nearby river.
   - **Key:** B — "let part of the land turn back into marsh" = allowed land to become wet again. "walls" is the trap word. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
4. Why have visitor numbers increased?
   - A  Earlier visitors recommended the centre.
   - B  There was a new advertising campaign.
   - C  Family tickets became cheaper.
   - **Key:** A — "families who came once and then told their friends" = recommendations. Advertising is rejected. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
5. Why did Daniel change his data collection?
   - A  His equipment broke.
   - B  The guide advised him to.
   - C  Low temperatures meant there were few insects.
   - **Key:** C — "so cold there weren't many about" = low temperatures, few insects. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
6. What does Daniel plan to do next?
   - A  Visit at a different time of year.
   - B  Write a report for the centre.
   - C  Study a different wetland.
   - **Key:** A — "go back in spring ... compare the two seasons" = a different time of year. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.

## L8 · Radio interview: the new Brookfield Library

`bh-ielts-targeted-listening-l8-v1` · Fresh check (independent evidence) · focus **Paraphrase recognition** · voices: Interviewer = Vincent, Ruth = Paula

**Student instructions:** You will hear a radio interview with Ruth Ellis, the manager of a local library. Answer questions 1 to 6. Choose the correct letter, A, B or C, for each question. This is a fresh check: listen once, without pausing, and answer on your own.

**Tip shown with the task:** The correct option expresses the speaker's meaning in different words.

**Transcript**

> **Interviewer:** Today I'm talking to Ruth Ellis, who manages Brookfield Library. Ruth, the library reopened last month. What's the biggest change?  
> **Ruth:** The building looks much the same, but people use it differently. The old rows of shelves were moved upstairs, so the ground floor is now open space where groups can get together.  
> **Interviewer:** Were people unhappy about losing the shelves?  
> **Ruth:** A few were at first. But not a single book was thrown away or sold. We still have the whole collection.  
> **Interviewer:** How was the work paid for?  
> **Ruth:** The council had no money for it, and we didn't want to put up our charges. In the end, local firms covered most of the bill.  
> **Interviewer:** And the opening hours?  
> **Ruth:** Weekdays and Saturdays are the same. The real difference is Sunday. We used to be closed all day; now we open from one until five.  
> **Interviewer:** What about young people?  
> **Ruth:** Teenagers told us there was nowhere peaceful at home to do their homework, so a room is reserved just for them after school.  
> **Interviewer:** Finally, what's next?  
> **Ruth:** We want to hear from the people who come here. A questionnaire goes out next week, and we'll plan the next stage around what they tell us.  

**Questions and key**

3. Who paid most of the cost?

   - C  Companies in the area.
   - **Key:** C — "local firms covered most of the bill" = companies in the area. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
4. What has changed about the opening hours?
   - A  It is now open for part of Sunday.
   - B  It opens later on weekdays.
   - C  It closes earlier on Saturdays.
   - **Key:** A — "now we open from one until five" on Sunday = part of Sunday. Primary: Paraphrase recognition; supporting: Distractor resistance.
5. Why is a room reserved for teenagers?

## L9 · Follow a process: making recycled paper

`bh-ielts-targeted-listening-l9-v1` · Guided practice · focus **Sequencing** · voices: Instructor = Miriam, Student = Noah

**Student instructions:** You will hear a conversation between an art instructor and a student about how to make recycled paper. Answer questions 1 to 6. Choose the correct letter, A, B or C, for each question. You may pause or replay for practice.

**Tip shown with the task:** The steps are not always mentioned in the order they happen. Listen for "before", "once", "only then", "after" and "last" to rebuild the real order.

**Transcript**

> **Instructor:** Today we're making recycled paper. Let me go through the process before you start. The very first thing is to tear your old paper into small pieces.  
> **Student:** Do we put the pieces straight into the blender?  
> **Instructor:** Not yet. Before blending, the pieces need to soak in warm water for about an hour. That softens them. Once they're soft, you blend them into a pulp.  
> **Student:** Can we add colour?  
> **Instructor:** Yes. Add a little food colouring while you're blending, not afterwards.  
> **Student:** And after blending, we pour it onto the frame?  
> **Instructor:** Almost. First you pour the pulp into a tray of water. Only then do you lower the frame into the tray and lift it out slowly, so a thin layer sits on the screen.  
> **Student:** Then we leave it on the frame to dry?  
> **Instructor:** No, that's a common mistake. Straight after lifting it out, you turn the frame over onto a cloth and press out the extra water with a sponge.  
> **Student:** And that's it?  
> **Instructor:** One more stage, and it's the last one. Hang the cloth on a line overnight to dry. In the morning, peel off your paper.  

**Questions and key**

1. What do students do immediately after tearing the paper?
   - A  Blend it.
   - B  Soak it in warm water.
   - C  Pour it into a tray.
   - **Key:** B — The student suggests the blender, but "Not yet. Before blending, the pieces need to soak". Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.
2. When should colour be added?
   - A  During blending.
   - B  Before soaking.
   - C  After pressing.
   - **Key:** A — "Add a little food colouring while you're blending, not afterwards." Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.
3. What happens immediately before the frame is lowered?
   - A  The pulp is blended.
   - B  The frame is turned over.
   - C  The pulp is poured into a tray of water.
   - **Key:** C — "First you pour the pulp into a tray of water. Only then do you lower the frame". Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.
4. What do students do immediately after lifting the frame out?
   - A  Leave it on the frame to dry.
   - B  Turn it over onto a cloth.
   - C  Hang it on a line.
   - **Key:** B — Leaving it on the frame is "a common mistake"; "Straight after lifting it out, you turn the frame over". Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.
5. What is the last stage?
   - A  Pressing with a sponge.
   - B  Lifting the frame.
   - C  Drying on a line overnight.
   - **Key:** C — "One more stage, and it's the last one. Hang the cloth on a line overnight". Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.
6. Which stage comes between soaking and pouring into the tray?
   - A  Blending.
   - B  Tearing.
   - C  Pressing.
   - **Key:** A — Soak → blend → pour into the tray. Primary: Sequencing; supporting: Distractor resistance.

## L11 · Choosing next term's modules

**Student instructions:** You will hear a conversation between two students, Emma and Raj, about their course options. Answer questions 1 to 6. Choose the correct letter, A, B or C, for each question. You may pause or replay for practice.

**Tip shown with the task:** Listen for how each speaker feels, not only what they talk about. Words like "but", "honestly", "I'm not convinced" and "fair enough" often signal the real opinion.

> **Emma:** Have you decided which options to take next term, Raj?  
> **Raj:** Almost. I'm definitely doing Urban Planning. The lectures last year were brilliant. I came out of every one full of ideas.  
> **Emma:** I liked them too, though I found the reading list far too long.  
> **Raj:** Fair enough. What about Statistics? It's optional.  
> **Emma:** I know people complain about it, but honestly, I think it's worth doing. Employers keep asking for data skills.  
> **Raj:** Hmm. I'm not convinced. I can't see myself ever using it in the kind of job I want.  
> **Emma:** And the group project? I'm a bit nervous. Last time, two people in my group did nothing.  
> **Raj:** I don't mind group work, as long as we choose our own groups. That's the part I'd insist on.  
> **Emma:** Then there's the field trip to Lisbon. It sounds amazing, but it's so expensive.  
> **Raj:** Is it, though? Flights are cheap in March, and the department pays for the hostel. I think it's reasonable.  
> **Emma:** Maybe. And what did you think of the new tutor, Dr Moss?  
> **Raj:** She clearly knows her subject, but she spoke so fast I couldn't take notes. I hope she slows down.  

**Questions and key**

   - C  They were hard to follow.
   - **Key:** A — "brilliant ... came out of every one full of ideas" = inspiring. "too long" was Emma's view of the reading list. Primary: Speaker attitude or opinion; supporting: Paraphrase recognition.
2. What is Emma's opinion of Statistics?

   - B  It will help her find work.
   - C  It repeats what she studied at school.
   - **Key:** B — "worth doing. Employers keep asking for data skills." She mentions others' complaints but disagrees. Primary: Speaker attitude or opinion; supporting: Paraphrase recognition.
3. What is Raj's attitude to Statistics?
   - A  He is keen to start.
   - B  He thinks it is badly taught.
   - C  He doubts it will be useful to him.
   - **Key:** C — "I'm not convinced. I can't see myself ever using it". Primary: Speaker attitude or opinion; supporting: Paraphrase recognition.
4. What is Raj's condition for group work?
   - A  Groups must be small.
   - B  Students must choose their own groups.
   - C  Tutors must check everyone's work.
   - **Key:** B — "as long as we choose our own groups ... I'd insist on". Primary: Speaker attitude or opinion; supporting: Paraphrase recognition.

   - B  It is too high.

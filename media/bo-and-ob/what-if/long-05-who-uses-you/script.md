# What If You Could See Who Uses You?

Social What-If, own point of view. A red string appears between you and everyone using you; thicker = takes more. Gold = two-way. ~6 min.

Voices: JESS ("Heyyy bestie! Miss you so much! Quick favour... airport? At four AM?"), CLAIRE ("Love that! Mind if I take it to the meeting? I'll mention you, obviously."), MAX ("Oh no. Forgot my wallet again! Next one's on me, yeah?"), MUM ("Oh! Hello, love. Is everything alright? You never just call.").

## Narration (N), in order
1. You wake up. You reach for your phone. And something is tied around your wrist.
2. A thin red string. And another. And another. Seven of them, running off your wrist, under the door, and out into the city.
3. You tug one. Somewhere across the city, somebody's phone lights up.
4. Overnight, a red string has appeared between you and every person who is using you.
5. The more they take from you, the thicker the string. And the people on the other end can see it too.
6. By tonight, you'll find out who's been using you, who you've been using, and the one string you'll never want to cut.
7. The first string twitches before you've even got out of bed. Your phone buzzes.
8. Jess. Your "best friend". The string from her wrist to yours is as thick as a rope.
9. You scroll up. The last six times she messaged you, she needed something. A lift. A loan. A plus-one. A lift.
10. You never noticed. Because every single message started with "Miss you so much".
11. You look at the string. Then at the phone. And for the first time in years, you type: No.
12. The string goes slack. Three dots appear. Then disappear. Jess doesn't reply.
13. An hour later, she posts a selfie from the airport. Somebody else drove her. Her string to them is brand new.
14. At work, the strings are everywhere. Running across desks, over the coffee machine, tangled in the printer.
15. The new guy has eleven strings. Everyone uses the new guy.
16. And then there's Claire. She smiles at you every morning. She says your ideas are brilliant.
17. The string between you is thick and bright red. You check the last three projects.
18. Your ideas. Her name on every slide.
19. In the meeting, she starts presenting. And the whole room can see the string running from her straight to your wrist.
20. Nobody says a word. But for once, the boss turns to you. "Why don't you take it from here?"
21. Claire's string doesn't snap. But for the first time... it goes thin.
22. Lunch with Max. He's funny. He's loud. He's...
23. It is never on him. There's a string from Max to you. And another to the waiter. And another to his mum.
24. Here's the thing nobody says out loud. Most people who use you aren't villains. They're just used to you saying yes.
25. By the afternoon, the whole world is staring at their wrists.
26. An influencer with three million followers has three million strings. Not one of them goes back.
27. A man on a dating app has forty matches. Forty strings. He's been telling all of them how lonely he is.
28. Landlords look like spiders.
29. Grandparents, it turns out, have more strings than anyone. Every grandchild. Every single one. And they don't mind at all.
30. And in millions of homes, people discover the thickest string in the house comes from the cat.
31. The cat does not care. The cat has never cared. The cat would like its dinner now.
32. The strangest thing? The people with the most strings tied to them weren't weak. They were the ones who never learned to say no.
33. So you grab the scissors.
34. Snip. The neighbour who borrows your ladder every single weekend.
35. Snip. The cousin who only calls on payday.
36. Snip. The group chat you're only in because you have a car.
37. Each one snaps back like a rubber band. And each time, your arm feels a little lighter.
38. By the evening, there's only one string left on your wrist. The cat's. You leave that one.
39. You sit down. For the first time all day, you feel free.
40. And then you look down. At your other hand.
41. There are strings on that one too. Five of them. Running out of you. To other people.
42. You're the one pulling.
43. One goes to your best friend, Sam. You've told Sam about every bad day you've had for three years. You can't remember the last time you asked about hers.
44. One goes to your sister. You only ever call her when you need her car.
45. One goes to your old boss. You haven't spoken since you needed that reference.
46. And the thickest one, by far... goes to your mum.
47. Every call this year started the same way. Mum, can you... Mum, could you... Mum, I need...
48. Every birthday you called late. Every visit you cut short. Every "I'll call you back" that you never did.
49. It turns out the easiest people to use... are the ones who love you too much to say no.
50. So you call her. Not because you need anything. Just to call.
51. You never just call. Four words. They hurt more than all seven strings put together.
52. You talk for an hour. About her knee. About her garden. About the neighbour's terrible new fence.
53. And while you talk, something happens to the string. It stops being red. It turns gold. And it starts to glow at both ends.
54. Because a string isn't the problem. A one-way string is.
55. Tomorrow, you'll call Sam, just to ask how she is. You'll pay for Max's lunch, once, and tell him it's his turn next.
56. Tonight, you fall asleep with a gold string on your wrist. And a cat on your chest.
57. The cat is still using you. You've decided that's fine.
58. Because being used isn't the worst thing in the world. Being used by someone who never even notices you... is.
59. So. Who's the thickest string on your wrist?
60. And be honest... whose wrist is yours tied to? Tell me in the comments. Then maybe call them. Just to call.

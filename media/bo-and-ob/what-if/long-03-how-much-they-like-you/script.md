# What If You Could See How Much Everyone Actually Liked You?

Social-experiment What-If, told from your own embarrassing point of view. Target 6:30–7:30.
N = narrator. Other names are character voices.

## Cold open
N: You wake up. Your partner rolls over, smiles, and says:
PARTNER: "Morning, you."
N: And above their head, glowing softly, is a number. Sixty-two.
N: Sixty-two out of a hundred.
N: Overnight, a number has appeared above every person on Earth. It shows exactly how much that person likes you. Right now. Live. And here's the part nobody is ready for: they can see yours too.
N: By midnight, you'll find out who's been faking it, who's secretly jealous of you, and the one person whose number never moves.

## 7:40 — The kitchen
N: Sixty-two. Is sixty-two good? It's more than half. It's a pass. It's... a C minus.
N: Then you notice they're staring just above your head. And whatever they're seeing, their face says it's lower.
PARTNER: "...Are you still annoyed about last night?"
N: You make them coffee. Sixty-five. You leave the cup in the sink. Sixty.
N: That's when you get it. The number isn't love. It's today. And nobody ever tells you how much today counts.

## 8:30 — The bus
N: The bus is silent in a way buses have never been silent. Nobody's on their phone. Everybody is looking up.
N: The woman across the aisle: eleven. You haven't done anything. Then you realise you're sitting in the seat she always takes.
N: But most people on the bus... have no number at all. Blank. They haven't formed an opinion. They weren't thinking about you.
N: Your whole life, you've worried what strangers think of you. Strangers weren't thinking.

## 9:15 — The office
N: Your boss beams at you.
BOSS: "Great to see you!"
N: Thirty-eight.
N: The colleague who says "love that idea" in every single meeting: twenty-four. Three years of compliments. Twenty-four.
N: And the quiet guy from IT, who has never said more than four words to you...
IT: "...Morning."
N: Eighty-seven. You have no idea what you did. He will never tell you.
N: By ten o'clock the office has a new rule nobody wrote down: don't look up. Then you volunteer to take the boring report nobody wanted, and your boss's number jumps to sixty-one.
N: Because a lot of what we call liking someone is really just: you're useful to me.

## 12:30 — Lunch with your best friend
N: You've known Sam since you were nine. Ninety-one. Of course. You relax.
N: Then you share your news. You got the flat. The one with the balcony.
SAM: "That's amazing! I'm so happy for you!"
N: And you watch it happen. Ninety-one. Eighty-four. Seventy-nine.
N: Sam sees you seeing it. The smile doesn't move.
N: This is the one nobody admits. You can love someone completely and still feel a little smaller when they win. Not instead of loving them. At the same time.
SAM: "Sorry. I am happy for you. I'm just... stuck."
N: Ninety-three. Higher than before. Remember that. It's going to matter.

## 15:00 — Mum's house
N: Your mum opens the door. Ninety-six. Perfect.
N: Then your brother walks in behind you. You both look up at the same time.
N: Ninety-eight.
N: Mum, who has said "I love you both exactly the same" on every birthday since you can remember, goes to put the kettle on. And stays in the kitchen for a very long time.
BROTHER: "Two points. I'll take it."
N: You laugh. And then you ask him what he sees above your head. For him. He tells you. It's higher than you would ever have said out loud.

## 17:00 — The family group chat
N: Five o'clock. The family group chat. Forty-three people. Nobody has ever left.
AUNTIE PAT: "Love you all!! Big hugs!!"
N: Auntie Pat. Eleven heart emojis, every single morning. Her number for you... thirty-four.
N: And Uncle Ray, who hasn't typed a single word in four years... ninety.
N: The loudest love in the chat had the lowest number. The quietest one had the highest.
N: So you message Uncle Ray. Just him. Thank you for always being there.
N: Three dots. Then one word.
UNCLE RAY: "Always."
N: Ninety-seven.

## 18:00 — The mirror
N: Back home, brushing your teeth, you see something you didn't know was possible. A number above your own head, in the mirror.
N: It's how much you like yourself.
N: Twenty-one.
N: Lower than your boss. Lower than the colleague who says "love that idea". Everyone who actually knows you... likes you more than you do.
N: That's the most hidden thing of all. Not because anyone told you to hide it. Because you never once checked.

## 19:00 — A first date, through a window
N: Seven o'clock. On the way to the party, you walk past a restaurant window. A first date.
N: He's leaning back, scrolling his phone, playing it cool. She's stirring her drink, looking bored.
N: What she'd see above his head... eighty-four. What he'd see above hers... eighty-six.
N: Two people who are crazy about each other, both working very hard to look like they're not. Because if you never show you care... nobody can ever turn you down.
N: Then she looks up. And sees his number.
HER: "Oh, thank God."
HIM: "Me too. Me too."
N: For the first time all evening... neither of them is pretending.

## 20:00 — The party
N: That evening, at a friend's party, everyone is terrified. They stand around the edges, smiling politely, numbers hovering at a careful fifty.
N: Then you trip over the dog and pour red wine down your shirt. The whole room turns.
N: And every number goes up.
N: It keeps happening all night. The man who admits he lost his job: up. The woman who confesses she hates the music: up. The guy telling his best story about his new car: down. Down. Down.
N: People don't like you for being perfect. They like you the second you stop pretending to be.

## 23:59 — Bed
N: Your partner is reading. Sixty-two this morning. You haven't looked since.
N: So you say the thing you haven't been saying for weeks. That you've been tired. That you're worried about money. That you didn't mean what you said last night.
N: You don't look up. You don't need to.
PARTNER: "...Thank you for telling me."
N: At midnight, every number on Earth goes out.
N: And downstairs, the dog. Who was a hundred when you woke up. A hundred when you came home late. A hundred when you tripped over him. Is still a hundred. He always was. He never needed a number.

## Outro
N: So: who do you think gives you a hundred? And whose number would you be too scared to look at? Tell me in the comments. And if someone came to mind while you watched this, send it to them. See what happens to your number.

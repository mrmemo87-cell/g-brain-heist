## A Long Campaign

In 1796, Edward Jenner tested whether material from cowpox could protect a person against smallpox. His work helped establish vaccination. Much later, the World Health Organization (WHO) led a worldwide effort against smallpox. WHO intensified its eradication programme in 1967. Teams used vaccination and surveillance: they protected people and looked for new cases. The last known naturally occurring case was recorded in Somalia in 1977. In 1980, WHO declared that smallpox had been eradicated. This means that the disease was no longer spreading naturally anywhere in the world. The result depended on coordinated work across countries, rather than on one scientist working alone.

Fact-check references: [1](https://www.who.int/health-topics/smallpox), [2](https://www.who.int/news-room/spotlight/history-of-vaccination/history-of-smallpox-vaccination).

## More Than Three Colours

Light-sensitive cells called cones help eyes detect colour. Typical human colour vision uses three cone types, which are sensitive to different ranges of light wavelengths. These ranges are commonly associated with red, green and blue. The visual system compares the signals from the cone types; a cone is not simply a tiny pot of coloured paint. A system using three cone types is called trichromatic. A system using four is called tetrachromatic. Hummingbirds have four cone types. Experiments have shown that they can distinguish colour combinations that humans cannot normally tell apart. In this text, 'tetra' refers to four types of colour-sensitive cone, not four colours in the whole world.

Fact-check references: [1](https://www.nei.nih.gov/research-and-training/research-news/nei-scientists-use-retinal-connectomics-study-color-vision-circuitry-relevant-mood-myopia), [2](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2020/06/15/wild-hummingbirds-see-broad-range-colors-humans-can-only-imagine), [3](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1919377117).

## Reading a Historical Estimate

It estimates that about 500 million people became infected, roughly one-third of the world's population at the time. The same account gives an estimate of at least 50 million deaths worldwide. These are historical estimates, rather than an exact count of every person. The passage identifies 1918-1919 as the period of worldwide spread. It does not give an exact percentage of the world's population who died.
